# 2004 presidential vote choice (Campbell and Monson 2008)

Respondents of the 2004 Election Panel Study used by Campbell and Monson
(2008) to study the effect of state gay-marriage-ban ballot initiatives
on the 2004 presidential vote. The outcome is abstained, voted for
Kerry, or voted for Bush; abstention is the base alternative and, as the
mixture of routine and occasional nonvoters, the inflated alternative of
Bagozzi and Marchetti (2017, Replication II), whose Table A.4 these data
reproduce. Rows are the 1,341 complete cases of that specification.

## Usage

``` r
data(pres2004)
```

## Format

A data frame with 1341 rows and 17 variables:

- vote:

  Factor: `abstain`, `kerry`, `bush`.

- educ:

  Education, 1 (elementary) to 9 (advanced graduate work).

- female, black, hispanic:

  Indicators.

- pid7:

  Party identification, 1 (strong Republican) to 7 (strong Democrat).

- bush_values:

  "Shares my values" describes Bush: 1 (not well at all) to 4 (extremely
  well).

- battleground:

  1 if in a presidential battleground state.

- south:

  1 if in a Southern state.

- gmb:

  1 if the state had a gay-marriage-ban initiative on the 2004 ballot.

- agegroup:

  Age cohort: 1 (18-29), 2 (30-54), 3 (55 and over).

- evangelical:

  1 if white born-again / evangelical Christian.

- catholic, other_religion, secular:

  Religious affiliation indicators.

- ideology:

  1 liberal, 2 moderate, 3 conservative.

- mobilization:

  Additive index (0-5) of campaign contacts.

## Source

Campbell, D.E. and Monson, J.Q. (2008). The religion card: Gay marriage
and the 2004 presidential election. *Public Opinion Quarterly*, 72,
399-419. As analyzed in Bagozzi, B.E. and Marchetti, K. (2017).
Distinguishing occasional abstention from routine indifference in models
of vote choice. *Political Science Research and Methods*, 5(2), 277-294.

## Provenance and terms

Taken from the replication archive of Bagozzi and Marchetti (2017),
deposited at the Harvard Dataverse
([doi:10.7910/DVN/3JR2YL](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/3JR2YL) , version
1.1, released under CC0 1.0): the file
`Replication II/replicationdata.dta` (md5
29ee7e0d84871d898fb19e1450b5c9a0) of `PSRM Replication Files.zip`, which
redistributes the Campbell and Monson (2008) replication data; the
rebuilding script `data-raw/make_data.R` of the development repository
keeps the analysis variables of the published specification and the
complete cases the published table uses. Original variable names: `vote`
from `vote_turn` (0 abstain, 1 kerry, 2 bush), `educ`, `female`,
`black`, `hispanic`, `pid7` from `party7`, `bush_values` from
`w3gbvalu2`, `battleground` from `presbatt`, `south`, `gmb` from
`gayban2`, `agegroup` from `agegroup2`, `evangelical` from `bornagn_w`,
`catholic`, `other_religion` from `other_rel`, `secular`, `ideology`
from `ideo`, `mobilization` from `w3mobidx`. The published interaction
terms are products of the bundled columns. The log-likelihoods (the
inflated model's -545.279 agrees with the printed -545.27 when
truncated) and the split-equation coefficients of Table A.4 reproduce
(and the outcome-stage coefficients the package's tests check); the raw
Vuong statistic of the inflated against the plain model,
`vuong(imnl_fit, mnl_fit)`, is 1.68 on these data (positive favours the
inflated model; two-sided p = 0.09).

## Examples

``` r
data(pres2004)
f <- vote ~ educ + female + black + hispanic + pid7 + bush_values + battleground + south + gmb +
  agegroup + evangelical + evangelical:gmb + catholic + catholic:gmb + other_religion +
  secular + secular:gmb + ideology + mobilization
m0 <- mnl(f, data = pres2004)
# \donttest{
m1 <- imnl(vote ~ educ + female + black + hispanic + pid7 + bush_values + battleground +
  south + gmb + agegroup + evangelical + evangelical:gmb + catholic + catholic:gmb +
  other_religion + secular + secular:gmb + ideology + mobilization |
  educ + agegroup + mobilization + secular, data = pres2004)
#> The inflated likelihood has 2 distinct local maxima among the ends of the search from 14 starts (log-likelihoods about -545.3, -550.3); the highest is reported. See object$search.
vuong(m1, m0)
#> Vuong test:  m1 = m1 [ Inflated multinomial logit (inflated alternative: abstain, logit split) ]
#>              m2 = m0 [ Multinomial logit ]
#> positive statistics favor m1
#> 
#>  correction statistic p_m1_better p_m2_better p_two_sided
#>         raw     1.680       0.047       0.953       0.093
#>         AIC     0.658       0.255       0.745       0.510
#>         BIC    -1.998       0.977       0.023       0.046
#> Note: The inflated multinomial logit and the multinomial logit are nested at the boundary phi = 1, where the normal reference does not hold for testing the uninflated model; boot_lr_test() calibrates the statistic. 
# }
```
