# 2006 Pennsylvania state House vote choice (Arceneaux and Kolodny 2009)

Survey respondents in two Pennsylvania state House districts after a
field experiment in which a liberal interest group endorsed the
Democratic candidate by door-to-door canvassing or by telephone
(Arceneaux and Kolodny 2009). The outcome is the reported vote:
abstained, Democratic candidate, Republican or other candidate, or
nonresponse. Abstention is the base alternative of the published
analyses and mixes routine nonvoters with occasional nonvoters, the
baseline-inflated outcome of Bagozzi and Marchetti (2017, Replication
I), whose Table A.3 these data reproduce. Rows are the 1,998 complete
cases of that specification.

## Usage

``` r
data(pa2006)
```

## Format

A data frame with 1998 rows and 10 variables:

- vote:

  Factor: `abstain`, `democrat`, `republican_other`, `nonresponse`.

- canvass:

  1 if the respondent received the canvassing treatment.

- phone:

  1 if the respondent received the telephone treatment.

- democrat, republican:

  Party identification indicators (independents are the omitted group).

- household:

  Number of household members.

- age:

  Age in years.

- female:

  1 if female.

- voted2004:

  1 if the respondent reported voting in 2004.

- district156:

  1 if in District 156 (open seat), 0 if in District 161.

## Source

Arceneaux, K. and Kolodny, R. (2009). Educating the least informed:
Group endorsements in a grassroots campaign. *American Journal of
Political Science*, 53, 755-770. As analyzed in Bagozzi, B.E. and
Marchetti, K. (2017). Distinguishing occasional abstention from routine
indifference in models of vote choice. *Political Science Research and
Methods*, 5(2), 277-294.

## Provenance and terms

Taken from the replication archive of Bagozzi and Marchetti (2017),
deposited at the Harvard Dataverse
([doi:10.7910/DVN/3JR2YL](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/3JR2YL) , version
1.1, released under CC0 1.0): the file
`Replication I/Least_Informed.dta` (md5
0a0964fc9183e1506e893695000675a2) of `PSRM Replication Files.zip`, which
redistributes the Arceneaux and Kolodny (2009) replication data; the
rebuilding script `data-raw/make_data.R` of the development repository
keeps the analysis variables of the published specification and the
complete cases the published table uses. Original variable names: `vote`
from `votechoice_dk` (1 abstain, 2 democrat, 0 republican_other, 3
nonresponse), `canvass` from `canvass_all`, `phone` from `phone_only`,
`democrat` from `sid_dem`, `republican` from `sid_rep`, `household` from
`hhcount`, `age`, `female`, `voted2004` from `vg04`, `district156` from
`d156`. The published interaction terms are products of the bundled
columns.

## Examples

``` r
data(pa2006)
table(pa2006$vote)
#> 
#>          abstain         democrat republican_other      nonresponse 
#>              731              579              563              125 
f <- vote ~ canvass + phone + democrat + republican + canvass:democrat + canvass:republican +
  phone:democrat + phone:republican + household + age + female + voted2004 + district156
m0 <- mnl(f, data = pa2006)
m0$loglik                                       # published -2110.59
#> [1] -2110.593
## the published BIMNL (Table A.3) is fitted in vignette("inflation")
```
