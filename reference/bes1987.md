# Vote choice and issue distances in the 1987 British general election (Glasgow 2001)

Respondents of the 1987 British General Election Study who voted
Conservative, Labour or Alliance, with their distance to each party on
seven issues and their social and economic characteristics (Glasgow
2001, following Alvarez, Nagler and Bowler 2000). One row per respondent
and party (the long layout); the classic example of the IIA debate in
political science and of the mixed logit as its remedy.

## Usage

``` r
data(bes1987)
```

## Format

A data frame with 6,393 rows (2,131 respondents times 3 parties) and 26
variables:

- id:

  Respondent identifier.

- party:

  Factor: `conservative`, `labour`, `alliance`.

- chosen:

  1 for the party voted for.

- defense, phillips, taxation, nationalization, redistribution, crime,
  welfare:

  Distance between the respondent and the party on the issue: the
  absolute difference between the respondent's self-placement and the
  party's mean placement by all respondents, on 11-point scales
  (defense: nuclear weapons; phillips: inflation versus unemployment;
  crime: civil rights versus fighting crime).

- south, midlands, north, wales, scotland:

  Region of residence.

- union:

  1 if a member of a trade union or staff association.

- public:

  1 if employed in the public sector.

- working_class:

  1 if in a working-class (manual, personal service or agricultural)
  occupation.

- female:

  1 if female.

- age:

  Age in years divided by 10.

- homeowner:

  1 if the household owns its residence.

- income:

  Family income, 1 to 12.

- education:

  Age at the end of continuous full-time education, divided by 10.

- inflation, taxes, unemployment:

  Perceived change since the 1983 election in prices, taxes and
  unemployment, 1 (increased a lot) to 5 (fallen a lot).

## Provenance and terms

From the replication archive of Glasgow (2001) at the Harvard Dataverse
([doi:10.7910/DVN/PCPVWB](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/PCPVWB) , version
1.0, released under CC0 1.0): the file `glasgow.data.asc` of
`glasgow.web.zip` (md5 ab0c27430d5b281eed1859ebb88b64bc), its 39 columns
read in the order of the archive's GAUSS program. The survey is the
British General Election Study, 1987: Cross-Section Survey (Heath,
Jowell and Curtice; UK Data Service SN 2568,
[doi:10.5255/UKDA-SN-2568-1](https://doi.org/10.5255/UKDA-SN-2568-1) ),
which the UK Data Service distributes under the Creative Commons
Attribution 4.0 licence; use of these data should cite it. The
rebuilding script is `data-raw/make_examples.R` of the development
repository. The multinomial logit of the paper's Appendix A, Table C
(log-likelihood -1477.56) reproduces.
